## Lessons Learned

- When delegating fact-finding to a subagent with explicit instructions to persist output (commit to a branch, write to a file), verify the persistence actually happened (`git log` on the branch, read the file) before trusting a "completed" status — a subagent can report success in prose while skipping the actual write step, especially if it delegates further to a nested agent of its own.
- A technical claim from an earlier research pass is a claim, not a fact, until re-verified at the moment a decision or action depends on it — copying it forward into a ticket or plan without a fresh check lets a wrong diagnosis propagate silently until execution finally surfaces the mismatch.
- In a multi-tool/multi-account environment (multiple GitHub accounts, multiple Vercel teams), don't assume the currently-authenticated identity has the scope a task needs — check first (`gh auth status`, list accessible teams) rather than discovering the 403 mid-task.
- Splitting a "plan, don't do" decision ticket from a paired "task" execution ticket (used throughout this wayfinder effort) worked well for letting a human approve a judgment call once ("go with your recommendations") and then separately, explicitly, authorize the mechanical execution ("run ticket N") — worth reusing as a default pattern for any multi-step improvement effort touching production systems.
